A · House lights

The hall is the hero. The real auditorium sits blurred behind the map; on load a light sweeps from the screen to the back row, lighting rows as it passes. Your held seats carry a red ring that burns down over the 10 minutes — the hold is visible on the seat itself, not just in a corner. Trade-off: the most cinematic and the most "Frontrow"; the blurred photo costs a little contrast on the cheapest tier, so tier colours must stay bright.

C · Blueprint

The hall as an architect's plan. Outlined seats on grid paper, a title block, mono labels; on load the rows plot in from left to right like a pen plotter. The countdown is a fuse burning along the rail. Trade-off: the most distinctive and the most "engineer" — it flatters the system-design story — but outlined seats are harder to read at a glance on a phone, so the mobile map would need filled seats.

D · Stage view

You're in the room. The venue photo fills the top; the map is laid in perspective below it so near rows are bigger, like looking down from the balcony. When someone else takes a seat, a ripple runs out from it — the two-tab moment made visible. A "view from here" card previews the section. Trade-off: the boldest and best for concerts and arenas; perspective costs tap precision on the far rows, so the phone version would flatten to 2D and keep only the ripple.
